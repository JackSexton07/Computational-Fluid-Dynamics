Ferrari 499P v2: force breakdown from surface pressure at iteration 1500 (pressure only).
Reference: Aref 1.6762 m2, U 40 m/s. Negative Cl = downforce. Axle loads from the moment balance
about the axles (front x = 0.956 m, rear x = 4.054 m), including the pitching moment of drag;
this method reproduces OpenFOAM's Cl(f)/Cl(r) for v2 (+0.243/-0.566 vs +0.252/-0.571).

                     total Cl   front axle   rear axle   floor+diffuser Cl
Original (corrected)  -0.382      +0.214      -0.596         -1.451
v2                    -0.323      +0.243      -0.566         -0.997
(The original's own Cl(f)/Cl(r) used the Mustang's CofR and lRef and are not valid.)

v2 front-axle load by component:
  floor + diffuser (front share)          -0.576
  front wheel arches                      -0.114
  dead-end nose duct                      -0.109
  nose/bonnet/fender tops                 +0.240
  canopy + upper body (x 1.3-2.7 m)       +0.306
  rest of nose (x < 1.3 m)                +0.266
  rear wing (pitches the nose up)         +0.078
  wheels, splitter, other                 +0.152
  total                                   +0.243

Dead-end nose duct (inlet between splitter and nose lip, closed back wall at x = 0.52 m):
  ceiling (facing down)       0.752 m2  Cp +0.74  Cl +0.319
  splitter top (facing up)    0.769 m2  Cp +0.95  Cl -0.420
  back wall                   0.225 m2  Cp +0.94  Cd +0.119
  net                                             Cl -0.097  Cd +0.097
Splitter underside: 0.844 m2 at Cp +0.32 (no suction).

Interpretation: the upper body makes lift, as on any car. On a real LMH car the front underbody
(splitter + duct through-flow venting over the bonnet) cancels it. In this visual model the duct
is a dead end, so it adds drag and the front underbody makes little downforce; the front axle lifts.
